# Dataset research for license plate recognition

Checked: 7 November 2026. This note distinguishes the team-supplied Kaggle dataset from other datasets for which access has not been obtained.

## Recommendation and current status

**The team supplied Marvin Tiele’s ELPD as a candidate for detector training.** Its synthetic images and German-style plates fit the initial detector-training scope. The Kaggle distribution states CC BY 4.0; independent real-image evaluation remains unresolved. The separate ELP 1.0 and THI datasets remain alternatives. ELP describes both plate locations and transcriptions; THI describes a larger street-scene detection dataset. For both, access and the amount of suitable Polish or German material remain to be confirmed. The team supplied a local ELPD copy and its annotations were inspected. The alternatives have not been obtained or inspected.

## Candidate: ELP 1.0

The [authors' dataset page](https://emirismail.github.io/) describes 4,744 images containing 5,000 plates from 20 European countries and annotations for plate bounding boxes, four corners, and plate numbers. It does **not** enumerate countries or provide Polish/German counts or a breakdown of single-line passenger plates. Suitability and image quality for our scope are therefore unverified. The [authors' ELP repository](https://github.com/Emirismail/ELP) additionally describes text files with bounding boxes and sequence labels, but the exact relationship of its linked download to the ELP 1.0 release and the contents of that download have not been verified.

### Access and terms

1. Read the [ELP license agreement](https://emirismail.github.io/data/agreement_to_elp.pdf) and have your university determine who is authorized to sign it. The form asks for an authorized official; students should not assume they can sign for the institution.
2. The dataset page asks applicants to complete the agreement and email it to the first author. The agreement itself lists three recipients; follow the current instructions in the agreement and dataset page. Ask whether the intended university coursework qualifies as non-commercial research, whether all team members can access the dataset under the agreement, and how to obtain it.
3. Before selecting it, ask the authors to confirm Polish and/or German ordinary single-line plate counts, annotation format and image splits, readability, and whether repeated vehicles or video sequences can be identified. Ask specifically about permission to publish derived labels, annotated examples, model weights and evaluation results.
4. Only after the required agreement and authorization, follow the access instructions provided by the authors; keep images and restricted annotations outside the shared repository unless the agreement explicitly permits otherwise. The linked Google Drive folder exists in the repository description, but actual downloadable contents and access were not verified and the link does not replace the agreement.

The [agreement](https://emirismail.github.io/data/agreement_to_elp.pdf) describes a non-commercial research license, an initial three-year term, restricted sharing with eligible same-institution colleagues, limits on copying and distribution, a citation requirement, and deletion on expiry if not renewed. Its precise terms govern; do not assume that course use, public examples, or redistribution are automatically permitted. It identifies the ELP 1.0 paper for citation (DOI: [10.1109/TITS.2025.3554986](https://doi.org/10.1109/TITS.2025.3554986)).

## Alternative: THI License Plate Dataset

The [Technische Hochschule Ingolstadt dataset page](https://www.thi.de/forschung/carissma/c-isafe/thi-license-plate-dataset/) describes more than 17,000 street/vehicle images and around 18,000 labeled plates, with varied lighting, weather and vehicle types. It presents the dataset as suitable for European license plate detection. The public page does not establish the number or registration country of suitable plates. The page does not state a license, downloadable archive, exact annotation format, OCR transcriptions, or Polish/German plate counts. These remain unverified.

The owner page requests inquiries to **Alessandro.Zimmer@thi.de**. A teammate can ask for access and academic-use terms, a sample and annotation specification, country/format counts, text-label availability, and rules for sharing derived materials and weights. Ask whether the original video or vehicle grouping is available for leakage-resistant train/test splits. No request has been sent.

## Other leads and limits

- [Appen European License Plate Detection Annotations](https://www.appen.com/data-catalog/image-video-sets/license-anno) describes JSON boxes for 38,000 images or frames sourced from Germany, France and Switzerland. The catalog does not establish an accessible free license, underlying-image permissions, German registration counts, or OCR text. Request both annotation and image terms before considering it; it may be a commercial offering.
- [EU License Plates Images on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/3967850) offers a downloadable archive of cropped plates, primarily German. Crops do not provide the vehicle-scene context needed for training our detector. The record does not display a usable license or establish transcription labels; confirm rights and annotations with the creator before use.
- [UniData's German preview](https://huggingface.co/datasets/UniDataPro/germany-license-plate-dataset) lists five sample images and plate-text metadata under a stated CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 license; the full dataset is advertised for purchase. Five samples are insufficient for detector training, and machine-readable detection boxes and suitability for our task have not been verified. Preview terms do not establish terms for the full dataset.

## Evaluation when text labels are missing

Once a dataset is licensed and acquired, reserve a test set before training. Keep identifiable repeats of a vehicle or capture sequence in the same split, and document how grouping was determined; if identity is unavailable, disclose that leakage cannot be ruled out. Select only readable, in-scope test plates for text evaluation and retain all applicable plates for detection evaluation. If plate text is absent, team members can manually transcribe the held-out images locally **only if the applicable agreement permits derived annotations**. Record image identifier, plate box, transcription, country/format if known, and an illegible flag; check transcriptions independently where practical. Normalize letters to uppercase and remove spaces and separators consistently with the project spec. Keep restricted transcriptions private unless the license allows sharing.

Report the number of test images and eligible plates, country coverage, selection and exclusions, matching criteria, and both detection performance and exact full-text accuracy. For end-to-end accuracy, use all eligible ground-truth plates as the denominator and count missed detections as incorrect; matched-plate OCR accuracy may be reported separately. Do not represent OCR accuracy on pre-cropped plates as end-to-end vehicle-image accuracy.

## Local data supplied by the team

Inspected 7 November 2026: the local `train-data/plates` directory contains 2,329 PNG images in each of three exports (YOLO, COCO and Pascal VOC), approximately 11 GB total. Three sampled image files were byte-identical across exports; the exports should not be treated as independent datasets. The directory is excluded by the repository's existing ignore rule. The team identifies the source as [Marvin Tiele’s Kaggle ELPD](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/tielemarvin/european-license-plate-dataset-elpd?resource=download). The [creator’s full-dataset listing](https://ko-fi.com/s/0ba23137be) describes 2,329 synthetic images, with European-style plates designed primarily around German conventions. The number and export formats match the supplied data, though the files were not independently compared to the online download. No license file was found locally and COCO license metadata is empty. The [Kaggle data card](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/tielemarvin/european-license-plate-dataset-elpd) states [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), permitting use including academic work with attribution and the other license conditions. Credit Marvin Tiele, link the dataset and license, and indicate modifications. The separate purchased listing describes its own terms; do not assume those apply to other distributions. Teammates can visit the Kaggle page and use its Download control, following any account instructions; a local copy is already present. The source page and local filenames/formats match, but byte-for-byte identity with the remote download was not checked.

The COCO export contains 2,948 plate boxes across the images; YOLO contains 2,947. There are 43 images without a YOLO label file: 42 have no COCO boxes and one (`filename_prefix_02098_.png`) has one COCO box. Treat the 42 as unverified negatives until visually checked, and reconcile the remaining discrepancy before training. The YOLO labels use class 0 only, although the configuration lists two differently spelled plate classes; their box values pass basic normalized-range and image-bound checks. The YOLO training list contains paths that do not match the provided folder layout, and the configuration provides no validation or test split. Prepare corrected generated splits and paths without changing the original data. Check split leakage across repeated or visually related images before evaluation.

The supplied annotation fields contain plate locations but no plate-text transcriptions or reliable country labels. After confirming permission to create derived labels, transcribe a held-out sample of readable in-scope plates for end-to-end evaluation. Detection training looks technically feasible after correcting the export issues, but annotation quality, exact country-format coverage and suitability for real photographs remain unverified. Because the creator describes the dataset as synthetic, separately test on licensed real-world images and report the distinction. The creator also warns that some difficult or distant plates are intentionally unannotated and that labeling mistakes can occur.
